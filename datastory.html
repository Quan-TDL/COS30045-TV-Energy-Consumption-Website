Buying your first TV? Start here

Shopping for your first TV can feel overwhelming. There are so many screen sizes and screen types in Australian shops, and it's hard to tell which ones actually matter, especially if you want to keep your electricity use down.

To help you out, I looked at government data on TVs sold in Australia. My advice is to work out your size first, then look at screen types. This guide walks you through it in that order.

Who is this guide for?

Anyone in Australia who is buying a TV for the first time and wants to feel more confident about their choice.

What will you learn?

Which TV sizes are most common, and how screen type relates to energy use once you've picked a size.

1. Find out which TV sizes are common

The first thing to ask is: How common is each size of TV?

Take a look at the chart below. TVs aren't spread evenly across all sizes. Most of them are clustered around certain sizes, which means you'll have a lot more choice in some sizes than in others.

So when you start shopping, expect the most choice around the sizes where the chart is tallest.

2. Compare screen types and energy use

The second question is: How does screen type affect energy consumption?

This chart shows the average labelled energy consumption of LCD, LCD (LED) and OLED TVs for small, medium and large screens.

The biggest thing to take from this chart is that larger TVs generally use more energy than smaller ones. A bigger screen will usually cost you more to run.

Screen type can also make a difference, but only compare screen types within the same size group. Different screen types aren't spread evenly across the sizes, so comparing them across all sizes at once wouldn't be fair.

This also means you shouldn't assume that one screen type is always the most energy efficient.

Screen size has a strong link to energy use, and screen type adds some extra differences within each size.

What I'd recommend

Here's how I'd approach buying your first TV. Start by thinking about what size suits your space and needs, rather than assuming a bigger TV is automatically better.

Remember that larger TVs generally use more energy. Once you've chosen a size, compare the screen types and the individual models within that size range.

The data doesn't show that you should always pick one screen type over another. Instead, look at size first, then check the energy use of the specific models you're comparing.

Final takeaway

Choose a suitable TV size first, then compare screen types and individual models before you buy.

Choosing a TV can be difficult

Buying a TV can be difficult for a first-time buyer because there are many different screen sizes and screen technologies available in the Australian market. With so many choices, it can be difficult to know where to start or which factors are important when considering energy consumption.

A useful first step is to understand which TV sizes are actually common in the market. Once a suitable size has been identified, we can then look at whether different screen technologies are associated with different levels of energy consumption.

Who is our audience?

This story is aimed at first-time TV buyers in Australia who want to better understand their available choices and make a more informed decision.

What do they want to know?

Which TV sizes are commonly available, and after considering the size, how does screen type relate to energy consumption?

1. What TV sizes are commonly available?

The first question is: How frequent is each size of TV?

The distribution of TV screen sizes is not even. Instead, TVs are concentrated around particular size ranges, suggesting that some screen sizes are much more common than others in the Australian market.

The chart also shows some less frequent areas around approximately 59 to 69 inches. These unusual areas are worth investigating rather than immediately assuming that they are errors in the dataset.

Overall, the market is concentrated around particular screen sizes rather than being evenly distributed across all possible sizes.

From TV size to energy consumption

Understanding which TV sizes are common helps a buyer narrow down their choices, but size is only one part of the decision.

Once a buyer has considered an appropriate screen size, the next question is:

Does the type of screen technology affect how much energy the TV uses?

This leads to the second part of our story, where we compare different screen technologies across different TV sizes.

2. How does screen type affect energy consumption?

The second question is: How does screen type affect energy consumption?

The chart compares the average labelled energy consumption of LCD, LCD (LED), and OLED TVs across small, medium, and large screen sizes.

The chart shows an important overall pattern: larger TVs generally have higher energy consumption than smaller TVs.

At the same time, energy consumption can also differ between screen technologies. However, these differences should be considered within the same size category because different screen technologies are not distributed equally across TV sizes.

This means that we should not simply conclude that one screen technology is always the most energy efficient.

TV size has a strong relationship with energy consumption, while screen technology can create additional differences within each size.

Recommendation

For a first-time TV buyer, the data suggests a simple approach. First, consider what screen size is suitable for your needs rather than assuming that a larger TV is automatically better.

The data shows that larger TVs generally consume more energy. After choosing a suitable size, buyers can compare the screen technologies and individual models available within that size.

The data does not suggest that one screen technology should always be chosen over another. Instead, it suggests that buyers should consider screen size first, followed by the energy consumption of the specific models they are comparing.

Final takeaway

Choose a suitable TV size first, then compare screen technologies and individual models to make a more informed choice.
